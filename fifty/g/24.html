A diyBMS v4 controller and cell module, proven on the bench

Heat the pad and the pin together for two seconds. Feed solder into the joint, not onto the iron. Take the solder away first, then the iron.

Strip 3 mm, twist the strands, and tin the end before you solder it to a pad.

Unplug USB and disconnect any battery before you solder or change a wire. Work on a surface that does not burn, and wash your hands after handling solder.

Before you switch it on

  1. Look. Every joint is a shiny cone. No solder touches a neighbouring pad. No stray strands of wire.
  2. These must not beep. Set the multimeter to continuity and probe each pair. A beep means a short: find it before you plug in.
    CELL_PLUSGND
  3. These must beep. Probe the two ends of every connection.
    CELL_PLUSAA pack + · Cell module BAT+
    CELL_MINUSAA pack - · Fuse 1
    CELL_MINUS_FUSEDFuse 2 · Cell module BAT-
    MODULE_TXController TX+ · Cell module RX+
    MODULE_TX_RETURNController TX- · Cell module RX-
    MODULE_RXCell module TX+ · Controller RX+
    MODULE_RX_RETURNCell module TX- · Controller RX-
  4. Plug in. Nothing gets warm, nothing smells. If something does, unplug at once and go back to step 2.
  5. Flash the bring-up test. It reports every part it can find before you load the real firmware.pio run -e bringup -t upload && pio device monitor

Vakt is built

Vakt is the first stage of a diyBMS v4 build: the ESP32 controller in a printed case, talking over its isolated loop to one v4.50 cell module that runs from three NiMH AA cells instead of a lithium pack. It is where you flash the firmware, check calibration and rehearse fault responses with an amount of stored energy that cannot hurt you, before any module touches a real cell.
